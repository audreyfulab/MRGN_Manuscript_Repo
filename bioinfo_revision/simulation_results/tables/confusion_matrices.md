# Confusion matrices -- simulated trios

Generated by `results_scripts/make_all_tables.R`. Do not edit by hand; rerun the script.

**Columns are the generating model, rows the inferred label.** The last column is precision (of everything given this label, the share that was generated that way) and the bottom row is recall (of everything generated under this model, the share given the right label). The bottom-right cell is overall accuracy.

Each table covers one sample size: 5 models x 3 effect sizes x 20 replicates, so every column totals 60 and every table 300.

Labels are coarse: MRGN's `M0.1` and `M0.2` are both reported as `M0`, the same collapse behind the `mrgn.*.correct.coarse` column. `Other` is a fitted trio that matched none of the eight M-topologies; it is never the right answer for any model, so its precision is blank rather than 0.

## MRGN

The diagonal is the coarse-correct count. Read the three arms against each other: the drop from `truth` to `CSq`/`CSa` is the cost of confounder selection rather than of MRGN.

## GMAC

GMAC is scored on the **T1-T2 edge**, not on the trio topology. Its statistic is the Wald test on the mediator coefficient in `outcome ~ mediator + treatment + confounders`, and that regression is symmetric in T1 and T2 -- under `V1 -> T1 -> T2` the reverse coefficient is nonzero too, so the trans test fires whenever the cis one does. GMAC has no mechanism for orienting the edge: across all 1,500 trios `Cis Mediated` and `Trans Mediated` were picked 2 and 3 times.

So the three edge-present calls (`Cis Mediated`, `Trans Mediated`, `Undirected`) are pooled and the table asks what GMAC can answer. `M0` and `M3` have no T1-T2 edge; `M1`, `M2` and `M4` do. This is the framing the manuscript already used -- `Manuscript/other/tablescraps/MRGN.GMAC.class.inference.50conf` reports GMAC the same way. The four-way call is still in `inference_gmac.csv` if it is wanted.

The tables are lopsided towards edge-present, and that is a failure of confounder selection rather than a property of the test: GMAC selects the trio's own common child about half the time at n = 670, and adjusting for a collider on T1 and T2 manufactures the edge. Given the true confounders instead, the same test calls `M0` and `M3` edge-absent.

## MR-GGI

MR-GGI is scored on the **T1-T2 edge** for a reason internal to the method rather than as a simplification of it. It is a Mendelian randomisation estimator: it fits T2 on T1 instrumented by V1 and tests whether that causal effect is nonzero. Nothing in that test looks at whether V1 also acts on T2 directly, so `M0` and `M3` are the same trio to it, as are `M1` and `M4`. The two-row question is the one it answers, and the right answers are the same as GMAC's.

MR-GGI gets a third row, and it is not the same row as MRGN's `Other`. `Weak instrument` means the first-stage F for `V1 -> T1` fell below `mrggi.min.F` (10, the conventional Staiger-Stock rule) and the edge was never tested. `mrggi.fields()` records those trios as `edge = "none"`, so reading the edge column alone would score an untested trio as a confident edge-absent call -- 570 of 1,500 trios, and 209 of 300 at n = 50. They are broken out instead, and count against accuracy: a trio whose edge was never called is a trio whose edge was not identified.

That row is the finding rather than an inconvenience. The weak-instrument rate is a property of the **trio design**, not of the estimator -- there is one variant, and MR-GGI needs it to be a strong instrument for the cis gene. It falls from 70% at n = 50 to 19% at n = 1000, so MR-GGI's small-sample behaviour here is governed by instrument strength rather than by the test. Neither MRGN nor GMAC has an analogous gate.

Only the cis -> trans direction is scored. The reverse estimate is in `inference_mrggi.csv` but is not an edge call: with a single instrument the test statistic reduces to the instrument -> outcome t-statistic and the exposure's first stage cancels out, so the reverse direction reports significance on trios where V1 does not instrument T2 at all. See `MRGGI_METHODS.md`.

**Read the `M2` and `M3` columns before the accuracy figure.** Every other model recalls 0.65-0.78 at n = 1000; those two recall 0.02 and 0.03, and they account for most of the gap between MR-GGI's accuracy and the other methods'. Both are the method behaving as specified:

- `M3` (`V1 -> T1`, `V1 -> T2`, no T1-T2 edge) is called **edge-present** 48 of 60 times. This is the exclusion-restriction violation MR cannot detect: V1 acts on T2 through a path that does not go through T1, and two-stage least squares attributes that association to a `T1 -> T2` effect. Horizontal pleiotropy is the known failure mode of single-instrument MR and `M3` is its textbook case, so this column measures it rather than exposing a defect in the tabulation.
- `M2` (`V1 -> T1 <- T2`, edge present but oriented `T2 -> T1`) is called **edge-absent** 47 of 60 times. Here MR-GGI is arguably right and the scoring is what is coarse: it tests the cis -> trans effect, which under `M2` genuinely is zero. It is penalised because the table asks only whether an edge exists, in either direction.

`M2` is scored the same way regardless, because the alternative is worse: the point of the two-row framing is that all three methods face identical rows, columns and right answers, and giving MR-GGI its own definition of correct would make its accuracy incomparable with the other two. GMAC's symmetric Wald test does find `M2`'s edge, and that difference between the methods is itself a result. Quote this caveat beside any MR-GGI accuracy figure.

## T1-T2 edge -- MRGN vs GMAC vs MR-GGI

The model-level tables above cannot be compared across the three methods: neither GMAC nor MR-GGI names a model. The T1-T2 edge is the question all three answer, so MRGN's call is collapsed onto the same two rows -- `M0` and `M3` mean no edge, `M1`, `M2` and `M4` mean an edge -- and all three are scored the same way. The GMAC and MR-GGI tables in the sections above are the other halves of this comparison.

Two of the three get a third row, for different reasons, and the distinction matters when reading the summary. MRGN's `Other` means `infer.trio()` fitted the trio but its four edge indicators matched none of the eight topologies -- 300 of 300 trios in the CS-alpha arm at n = 50. MR-GGI's `Weak instrument` means the first-stage F for `V1 -> T1` fell below 10 and the edge was never tested -- 209 of 300 at n = 50. Neither is a wrong edge call; both are **no** edge call, and both count against accuracy, since a trio whose edge was never called is a trio whose edge was not identified. GMAC has no such row: it always answers, which is its structural difference from the other two.

MR-GGI selects no confounders at all -- the instrument is what is supposed to handle confounding, which is the method's premise -- so it has a single row rather than an arm per selection rule.
